# Kentucky rules for businesses that let someone else answer the phone

*General information, not legal advice. Have a Kentucky attorney confirm anything you rely on.*

## Recording calls

Kentucky's eavesdropping statute defines to "eavesdrop" as to "overhear, record, amplify or transmit any part of a wire or oral communication of others without the consent of at least one (1) party thereto by means of any electronic, mechanical or other device" ([KRS 526.010](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=19948)). Eavesdropping is a Class D felony ([KRS 526.020](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=19949)). A business that answers a call is a party to it, so recording its own calls is consistent with that language.

Two cautions. First, a caller in another state may be covered by that state's rules; roughly a dozen states require every party to consent, and sources disagree on the exact list. Second, many businesses play a short notice anyway, such as "this call may be recorded."

## AI voice

We found no Kentucky statute that requires a private business to tell callers they are speaking with an AI. Kentucky's Attorney General sued an AI chatbot company in January 2026 under the state's consumer-protection law, which shows existing law can reach deceptive AI conduct. Some other states have passed AI-disclosure rules. The safe practice is to tell callers up front.

Federal law adds one point. In 2024 [the FCC ruled](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf) that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, so calls an AI system places need the person's prior express consent. Answering inbound calls is a different situation from placing outbound calls.

## Which trades need a state license

The state's license table (Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction) shows:

| Trade | State license required |
| --- | --- |
| Plumbing | Yes |
| Electrical | Yes |
| Heating, ventilation and air conditioning | Yes |
| General erection and construction | No state license required |
| Sprinklers and suppression, boilers, elevators | Yes |

Roofing, landscaping and tree work do not appear in the table excerpt we reviewed. Local city and county rules vary; check yours.

If your phone is answered by someone else, they should not quote prices, diagnose problems, or promise a licensed technician unless you have given them the words to use.
