# Missed calls for Kentucky roofing contractors: the market by county

## Why this trade is different

Roofers are on a roof, not by a phone. Calls arrive in two forms: a leak or storm-damage call from someone who wants a response quickly, and a replacement-estimate call from a homeowner collecting several bids. In the second case the company that calls back first often gets the inspection. For how many home-service calls go unanswered, see [How many calls do home service businesses miss?](/guides/missed-calls-research).

## The Kentucky market, by county

[Census County Business Patterns (2023)](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/data/api.html) counts **219** roofing contractor establishments with paid employees, and reports a count in only **24** of Kentucky's counties. A county with no count is not necessarily a county with no roofers: it may have none with paid employees, or the Census may not report it, and the data excludes owner-operators with no payroll, which are common in roofing.

Only Jefferson and Fayette counties have 20 or more establishments, 14 counties have between 5 and 19, and 8 have fewer than 5. Because the data is thin outside the two largest counties, a statewide roofing page is more useful for what it says about the trade than for county rankings.

## What an answered call has to do in this trade

- **Leaks and storm damage.** Is water coming in now, is any part of the structure unsafe, can the caller place a tarp or move belongings? Capture the address and a callback number, and say what happens next and when.
- **Estimate requests.** Address, roof age if known, the type of work, whether an insurance claim is involved, and a time for an inspection.
- **No pricing or guarantees by phone** unless you have given the person on the phone the words to use.

Kentucky's [state license table](https://dhbc.ky.gov/Documents/KHBC_Plan%20Submission%20Application%20Guide%2025-0418.pdf) lists general erection and construction as "no state license required" and does not list roofing; requirements can differ by city and county, so check yours. Recording and AI-voice rules are covered in [Kentucky rules for businesses that let someone else answer the phone](/kentucky/call-rules).
