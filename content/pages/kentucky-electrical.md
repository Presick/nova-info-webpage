# Kentucky electricians: why quote calls and urgent calls go to competitors

## The problem: two kinds of calls, and one phone

Electrical calls mix routine jobs, such as a panel upgrade quote or a new circuit, with calls that carry real risk: a burning smell, a warm outlet, a partial power loss. The routine calls are quotes homeowners shop around, so the first company to answer often gets the estimate visit. The urgent calls need a person or a system that knows when to say "hang up and call 911." Either way, a call that goes to voicemail is a call that goes to someone else.

## What the numbers say

- **A panel job is a real ticket.** Angi's 2026 data, drawn from 9,551 verified projects reported by Angi pros, puts panel replacement between about **$518 and $2,193**, with averages cited near **$1,343**. A 200-amp upgrade is estimated at $1,200 to $2,000, and relocation or service-entrance work can push totals higher ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-replace-circuit-breaker-box.htm), [Angi 200-amp guide](https://www.angi.com/articles/ask-angie-what-does-it-cost-upgrade-200-amps.htm)). Older HomeAdvisor figures sit near $1,100 to $1,300 and are stale.
- **Home-service calls go unanswered.** Published studies put the unanswered share between about 14 percent and about three in four, depending on who measured and how. They come from companies that sell phone tools, so read the spread as a range ([summary and worksheet](/guides/missed-calls-research)).
- **Speed decides quote jobs.** Homeowners ask several contractors for quotes and tend to hire the one who responds first, although the famous response-time multipliers are shaky ([what the research says](/guides/speed-to-lead-contractors)).

Use your own call log and average ticket to size the loss.

## Why electrical calls get missed

1. **Electricians work in panels and attics**, not next to a phone.
2. **Quote calls come in clusters** during business hours, while crews are on jobs.
3. **Urgent calls arrive at night**, and voicemail is the wrong answer to a burning smell.
4. **Open quotes are never chased**, and the homeowner books whoever follows up.

## What an answered call has to do in this trade

- **Safety first.** A caller who reports smoke, sparks, a burning smell or shocks should be told to leave the area and call 911, not to wait for a callback.
- **Sort routine from urgent.** Panel upgrades, EV chargers and lighting quotes can be booked for a visit; a partial power loss or a tripping main breaker may need same-day dispatch.
- **Capture the details an estimator needs.** Address, service type, whether the home is occupied, and a callback number.

Ask any answering service or AI receptionist what it says to a caller who mentions sparks or a burning smell, and whether that script is yours to edit. Kentucky requires [a state license](https://dhbc.ky.gov/Documents/KHBC_Plan%20Submission%20Application%20Guide%2025-0418.pdf) for electrical work, so the person on the phone must not promise a licensed electrician or give electrical advice beyond safety instructions you approve.
