# Missed calls for Kentucky electrical contractors: the market by county

## Why this trade is different

Electrical calls mix routine jobs (a panel upgrade quote, a new circuit) with calls that carry real risk (a burning smell, a dead outlet that is warm, a power loss in part of a house). The routine calls are quotes and consultations that homeowners shop around, so the first company to answer often gets the estimate visit. The urgent calls need a person or a system that knows when to say "hang up and call 911." For how many home-service calls go unanswered, see [How many calls do home service businesses miss?](/guides/missed-calls-research).

## The Kentucky market, by county

[Census County Business Patterns (2023)](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/data/api.html) counts **855** electrical contractor establishments with paid employees across the 68 Kentucky counties where a count is reported. It does not count owner-operators with no payroll.

Eight counties have 20 or more electrical establishments, 39 have between 5 and 19, and 21 have fewer than 5. Kenton County, in Northern Kentucky, is third in the state at 42, ahead of Warren, Hardin and Boone.

## What an answered call has to do in this trade

- **Safety first.** A caller who reports smoke, sparks, a burning smell or shocks should be told to leave the area and call 911, not to wait for a callback.
- **Sort routine from urgent.** Panel upgrades, EV chargers and lighting quotes can be booked for a visit; a partial power loss or a tripping main breaker may need same-day dispatch.
- **Capture the details an estimator needs.** Address, service type, whether the home is occupied, and a callback number.

Ask any answering service or AI receptionist what it says to a caller who mentions sparks or a burning smell, and whether that script is yours to edit. Kentucky requires [a state license](https://dhbc.ky.gov/Documents/KHBC_Plan%20Submission%20Application%20Guide%2025-0418.pdf) for electrical work, so the person on the phone must not promise a licensed electrician or give electrical advice beyond safety instructions you approve. Recording and AI-voice rules are covered in [Kentucky rules for businesses that let someone else answer the phone](/kentucky/call-rules).
