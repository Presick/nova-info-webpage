# Kentucky landscaping and tree services: why estimate and storm calls go unanswered

## The problem: loud equipment, one phone, and a customer who keeps dialing

Crews in this trade work with loud equipment, in the field, and away from the phone for most of the day. Two kinds of calls come in. Estimate calls, such as a bid for lawn care, a hardscape quote or a seasonal contract, are shopped around, and the company that responds first often gets the site visit. Storm and hazard calls, such as a limb on a driveway or a downed tree, are urgent.

## What the numbers say

- **Tree jobs are worth the call.** Published cost guides put a standard tree removal at about **$750 to $906**, with smaller trees from $150 to $450 and the largest well into the thousands. Emergency or storm removal is typically **$1,000 to $2,500**, and a tree on a house or power line can run **$1,000 to $5,000 or more**. Guides disagree on how big the emergency premium is, so use these as ranges ([This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/gardening/tree-removal-cost), [emergency cost guide](https://costtoremoveatree.com/emergency-tree-removal-cost/)).
- **Few callers leave voicemail.** Landscaping answering-service vendors report that very few callers leave a message, with figures ranging from under 3 percent to under 15 percent. These are vendor claims we could not trace to a primary study ([Callin](https://callin.io/answering-service-for-landscapers/), [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai/blog/how-landscaping-companies-handle-calls-during-busy-season)).
- **Spring is the crunch.** Vendors say missed calls climb during the March to May clean-up rush. We could not confirm the percentage, so we leave it out. Count your own spring call log.
- **Homeowners shop around.** The first company to respond tends to win estimate jobs, though the exact multipliers repeated online are unreliable ([what the research says](/guides/speed-to-lead-contractors)).

Published studies of home-service calls put the unanswered share between about 14 percent and about three in four, depending on who measured and how ([summary and worksheet](/guides/missed-calls-research)).

Employment per establishment is low in this trade: Jefferson County has 241 establishments and 1,761 paid employees, about seven per establishment, so most of these companies are small crews. A small crew is the least able to stop and answer a phone.

## Why landscaping and tree companies miss calls

1. **Mowers, chainsaws and chippers** drown out the ring.
2. **The owner is also the crew lead**, so nobody is in the office.
3. **Storm calls bunch up** after wind and ice, and one phone can only take one at a time.
4. **Bids are never chased.** Seasonal and commercial inquiries are time-sensitive.

## What an answered call has to do in this trade

- **Estimate calls.** Capture the address, the size of the property, the service wanted, and a good time for a site visit. Seasonal and commercial inquiries are often time-sensitive.
- **Storm and hazard calls.** The first question is safety: is anyone hurt, is a power line involved? A caller who reports a line down should be told to stay away and call 911 or the utility, not to wait for a crew.
- **Photos.** If the company uses text messages to collect photos of damage, ask the vendor how it handles consent for texts.
