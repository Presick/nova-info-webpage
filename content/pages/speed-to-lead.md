# Speed to lead for contractors: why the first company to respond wins the job

## The problem: homeowners do not wait

A homeowner with a leaking roof or a dead air conditioner does not ask one contractor. They call or message several, and they tend to hire the one who answers and books first. Every hour of delay and every call that goes to voicemail is a chance for someone else to win the job.

## What the research says

- **Slow response is common.** A 2011 Harvard Business Review audit of 2,241 U.S. companies, as summarized by Workato, found that among firms that replied to an online lead at all, the average response time was 42 hours, and 23 percent never replied. Some summaries give a range of 42 to 47 hours. This audit covers online sales leads, not phone calls. Source: [Workato](https://workato.com/the-connector/lead-response-time-study).
- **The famous multipliers are shaky.** The "21 times more likely to qualify within five minutes" figure traces to a study led by James Oldroyd with InsideSales.com, a company that sells speed-to-lead software. The "391 percent" figure traces to a 2012 Velocify report, and some blogs have merged these numbers. Read them as directional. Source: [Workato](https://workato.com/the-connector/lead-response-time-study).
- **Roofing vendors report slow contractors.** Roofing software vendors say the average roofing contractor takes about 42 hours to respond to a new lead and that homeowners typically contact three to five companies at once. We could not trace these to a primary study. Sources: [JobNimbus](https://jobnimbus.com/blog/speed-to-lead-the-simplest-way-to-win-more-roofing-jobs-without-more-leads), [Roofr](https://roofr.com/blog/roofing-sales-metrics-to-track).
- **Responsiveness matters to buyers.** Roofr reports that 74 percent of homeowners in its survey say responsiveness and communication matter more than price. Source: [Roofr](https://roofr.com/blog/roofing-sales-metrics-to-track).

The honest summary: precise multipliers are unreliable, but several independent sources agree that fast response wins jobs and slow response loses them.

## Where contractors lose the race

1. **Missed calls.** Published studies put unanswered home-service calls between 14 percent and about three in four, depending on how they count ([our summary](/guides/missed-calls-research)).
2. **After-hours inquiries.** Nobody is there to answer, and the lead moves on.
3. **Estimates with no follow-up.** A quote sent on Monday and never chased is a quote that expires.
4. **Overloaded offices.** Storms and temperature swings send a spike of calls that one or two people cannot handle.
