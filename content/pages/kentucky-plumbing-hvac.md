# Kentucky plumbers and HVAC contractors: how missed emergency calls cost you jobs

## The problem: emergencies come when nobody can pick up

A burst pipe, no heat, a failed air conditioner. These calls cannot wait, and they arrive when your techs are under a house, in an attic, or home for the night. A caller who gets voicemail has little reason to leave a message and plenty of reasons to dial the next company.

## What the numbers say

- **Home-service calls go unanswered.** Published studies put the unanswered share between about 14 percent (CallRail, January 2025) and about three in four (a vendor analysis of 130,175 calls to 45 contractors, where even the best trade, plumbing, missed 28.6 percent). Sources and caveats are in our [missed-call summary](/guides/missed-calls-research).
- **A plumbing emergency is not a small job.** HomeAdvisor lists an average burst pipe repair of about $500, most between $200 and $3,000, plus emergency call-out fees of $100 to $300 ([HomeAdvisor](https://homeadvisor.com/cost/plumbing/repair-burst-pipe/)).
- **HVAC is bigger.** Ainora, citing HomeAdvisor and Angi, lists an average HVAC repair of about $340 and central AC replacement at $5,000 to $12,000 ([Ainora](https://ainora.lt/blog/hvac-service-call-statistics-2026)).

Read these as a range. Count your own missed calls over a rush week and use your own average ticket.

For deeper dives, see [plumbers and after-hours emergency calls](/plumbing/after-hours-emergency-calls) and [HVAC missed calls in the summer and winter rush](/hvac/peak-season-missed-calls).

In a county with only a handful of competitors, being the one that answers matters more than being the cheapest.

## Why Kentucky plumbers and HVAC companies miss calls

1. **Techs are in the field**, and the office is small or empty after hours.
2. **Peak weather sends the same emergency to every company at once.**
3. **Voicemail does not book anything**, and the caller keeps dialing.
4. **Estimates go cold** when no one follows up on a replacement quote.

## What an answered call has to do in this trade

- **Triage.** Is water still running, is there gas, is there no heat for someone vulnerable? The right first step is often "shut off the main valve" or "call 911", not "leave a message."
- **Dispatch rules.** Who is on call tonight, what is the after-hours rate, and what can wait until morning.
- **Booking.** A time on the calendar, an address, and a number to confirm.

Ask any answering service or AI receptionist exactly what it says to a caller with an active leak or a gas smell, and who it contacts. Kentucky requires [a state license](https://dhbc.ky.gov/Documents/KHBC_Plan%20Submission%20Application%20Guide%2025-0418.pdf) for plumbing and for heating and air-conditioning work, so the person on the phone must not promise a licensed technician unless you have told them to.
